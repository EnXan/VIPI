# Decision-model review and confidence validation for OCR recovery

Date: 2026-11-07. Initial review at `4e3f6096`; point-4 trial completed in the
working tree on 2026-11-07.

This report records research findings and a scoped live trial. It does not
itself change production policy, model weights or reference annotations. The
trial used the current working-tree recovery implementation and incurred an
estimated $0.85635 in known provider usage.

The product objective discussed here is complete transcription of extensive
visible text. Decision models remain useful candidates for routing and comparing
OCR attempts, but their benefit on this objective needs direct measurement.

## How the models relate to this task

[Liquid's pinned d1-3B release](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-open) describes
decision inference without autoregressive text generation. The
[Clef model card](https://huggingface.co/Cloudflare/clef) describes a multimodal
backbone and a schema head scoring allowed answers. These mechanisms support
classification and candidate selection; an answer probability is not a measured
fraction of image text recovered. General decision benchmarks do not validate
OCR completeness assessment on the VIPI images.

The [OpenAI Decisions guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions#ask-multiple-questions)
recommends observable criteria, separate questions for distinct concerns, and
application labels for threshold selection. Its choice probabilities and
`confidence` are separate fields. They must not be treated as interchangeable
or assigned a universal routing threshold.

Current [Liquid documentation](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/models/decision-models)
also describes newer models and additional typed outputs. That does not establish
support for those features in VIPI's pinned d1-3B runtime. No model migration or
new output primitive is proposed here; existing named choice questions suffice
for the initial experiment.

## Findings in the current implementation

| Location | Observed behavior | Implication to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `decisions.py`, `_AdaptiveDecider.assess()` | One choice combines `ready`, orientation, contrast and uncertainty. The question asks whether an image transformation is needed, and permits stopping when OCR is usable. | Lack of an obvious transformation can be confused with acceptable completeness. Several defects can coexist. |
| `decisions.py`, `decision_preview()` and `LocalDecider._ask()` | Assessment previews have a 1024-pixel maximum side. Local multi-image requests additionally cap each view at 512 pixels, documented as a memory workaround for measured 16-GB MPS failures. | Small text may become unreadable to the reviewer. This is a plausible mechanism, not an isolated cause established by the benchmark. |
| `decisions.py`, `_AdaptiveDecider.compare()` | Local review receives group text and global images; hosted review additionally receives group boxes and an OCR-derived text-region crop. | Region-aligned differences could simplify comparisons. Existing OCR-derived crops cannot expose all regions missed by OCR. The original global image is already supplied. |
| `decisions.py`, `read_choice()` | Confidence is validated as a finite number in [0, 1]; the helper then returns the chosen label. Probabilities and confidence do not govern acceptance here. | Acceptance is not calibrated to the cost of false readiness or a false improvement. |
| `pipeline.py`, `_correction_plans()` | Orientation and contrast are alternative routes. `ready`/`uncertain` yield no plans from this function; later resolution/tile stages may still run. | Poor completeness, unknown repair and successful transcription are not distinct outcomes. Orientation-plus-contrast is not generated by this router. |
| `pipeline.py`, `_select_reading()` | The first model-judged improved correction is retained. | A later generated candidate may be better; inspecting existing alternatives can measure the selection opportunity before expanding inference. |
| `probes.py`, `recover_resolution()` | A guarded 2x comparison and spatially supported 1.5x letter insertions are separate mechanisms. | Letter insertion is not a mechanism for discovering an entirely absent paragraph. Their contributions should be measured separately. |
| `tiles.py`, `recover_with_tiles()` | Tiling is triggered by image size or already-detected small text. Acceptance checks confident tokens and an unordered word multiset. | This does not certify completeness, reading order or coordinate fidelity. A spatial regression must not automatically be described as lost transcript words. |

These paths are under [the recovery package](../../../src/vipi/ocr/recovery).
The provider API contract and the application routing policy are different
layers; improving one does not prove the other correct.

## What existing experiments already establish

- [Signal-informed assessment](OCR_SIGNAL_CONTEXT.md) supplied additional signal
  evidence and OCR-derived crops. Full-suite accounting remained **92/113**,
  with no change in matched words and additional work. That change was reverted
  at the user's request. Simply restoring it is not the proposed experiment.
- [Missing-text experiments](OCR_MISSING_TEXT_EXPERIMENT.md) used disagreement
  between existing OCR attempts to suggest new regions. Tight crop retries did
  not recover the previously missing reference words on the two triggered
  failing cases. The method could not find text missed by both attempts.
- [OCR-first comparison](OCR_PAIR_COMPARISON.md) exposed a genuine selection
  opportunity: a 15/15 mirror reading existed but was rejected against a 5/15
  incumbent. Other cases had no useful candidate at all. Selection failure and
  candidate-generation failure therefore need separate measurements.
- [Book-spine diagnosis](BOOK_SPINE_DIAGNOSIS_2026-11-02.md) found sparse detection,
  an untriggered local review route and an optimistic forced review. Reference-
  guided recognizer controls also remained weak. A better reviewer is not
  guaranteed to recover text that the OCR backend cannot read.

These are development findings with different historical protocols. They do
not establish a general success rate for a proposed replacement.

## Proposed adjustments and their validation

**1. Separate completeness from repair selection.** Ask whether readable text
is visibly missing, whether supplied detail is sufficient to judge it, and
whether a particular repair is supported. Use independent named choice
questions with distinct outcomes, including inability to judge. Derive the action
in ordinary code. Do not ask the model to invent a precise coverage percentage
without a reference, or treat uncertainty as successful completion. Require a
separate test of whether readiness can identify the existing 80%/100% objectives.

**2. Make dense text visible during review.** Test a whole-page overview together
with region crops retaining source detail. Include page regions beyond detected
OCR boxes; otherwise the model is still blind to shared detection misses.
Use explicit region IDs and source-coordinate mappings. Preserve context around
lines and overlapping boundaries. The
[Mini-Monkey study](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02034v3) motivates combining
detail and context and warns that cropping can split connected text. It studies
other model architectures, so it supports a hypothesis, not a proven VIPI fix.
Measure memory and latency on the actual local runtime instead of removing the
512-pixel workaround blindly or adding an arbitrary crop-count cap.

**3. Compare the changed text at the same locations.** Give both candidates the
same source region and stable IDs; ask separately about recovered text, damaged
existing text and local reading order. Preserve a whole-page coverage check so
unchanged or newly visible areas are not excluded. Do not reward length alone or
protect every incumbent error so strongly that legitimate corrections are blocked.
Test A/B order reversals as a diagnostic before adding duplicate comparisons to
production. [Judge research](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4) motivates this
control but is not an OCR result. A recent
[decision-model preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29217v1) also reports
schema/order sensitivity for d1 and Luna on text tasks. It is not peer-reviewed
evidence of the same bias in our visual requests.

**4. Calibrate action policy after improving the evidence.** Evaluate labels,
per-option probabilities and confidence separately for each provider/schema.
Use disjoint calibration and evaluation images. General calibration research
such as [Guo et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) supports
checking reliability; it does not establish temperature scaling as a remedy for
VIPI's visual omissions. Calibration cannot restore pixels that were removed or
create a useful OCR candidate. Avoid an invented `0.8`/`0.9` decision threshold.

**5. Validate content, order and geometry independently.** Reproduce the known
tile/assembly regressions before changing the reviewer. Word-multiset retention
can pass despite a reading-order or localization regression. Preserve native
word provenance and test round-trip coordinate mapping, overlap deduplication
and reconstruction of the selected text. Decide a repair from that diagnosis;
do not hide the regression by replacing the spatial metric.

## Smallest useful next experiment

First audit the saved attempts offline: compare initial, selected and best
available candidate with the same evaluation rules. The best available candidate
is an evaluation-only ceiling for that candidate pool, not a global OCR ceiling.
This distinguishes potential selection gains from missing OCR capability without
new inference.

The first proposed live experiment is a controlled completeness-assessment
comparison using the same frozen image/OCR pairs:

| Arm | Question schema | Visual input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Current | Current previews |
| B | Separate completeness question | Current previews |
| C | Current | Overview plus source-detail regions |
| D | Separate completeness question | Overview plus source-detail regions |

This factorial comparison isolates question changes, evidence changes and their
interaction. It reuses saved OCR, so no new OCR inference is needed initially;
new model assessments still incur latency and any provider fee. Keep failures,
refusals and unjudgeable images in the accounting. Reference-guided region crops
must not become model inputs. Do not restore the reverted signal-context mode
or run a large transformation sweep as an implicit part of this experiment.

Freeze extensive-text failures plus genuinely good controls covering columns,
small text, tables and mixed orientation. Check the reference scope and visually
review ambiguous legibility before assigning assessment labels. Separate tuning,
calibration and evaluation by image/content groups so duplicate images cannot
cross splits. Existing TextOCR imports come from training annotations; such a
local split is not proof of independence from model pretraining.

Measure false readiness below 80% coverage and below full reference coverage,
unnecessary retries on good controls, inability to judge, candidate-selection
regressions, tolerant/exact word coverage, reading order and spatial integrity,
plus added latency and API fees. For candidate selection, also measure the gap
to the best existing candidate. Report image-level uncertainty for paired gains.
Do not declare improvement solely because the model reports higher confidence.

Review each concept before production adoption. A same-input comparison with
strong OCR references remains necessary to estimate the attainable quality gap;
this review does not establish that VIPI is near or far from state of the art.

## Point 4: scoped cloud confidence trial

The first point-4 trial ran only Cloud-default on 100 category-stratified
entries selected from the union of previous local/cloud full-reference failures
and unscored outputs. All 100 selected entries have distinct image hashes: 81
are TextOCR and 19 cover seven other categories. The preassigned image-hash
split has 79 calibration and 21 evaluation entries. The frozen selection and
full method are retained in the [selection JSON](../experiments/DECISION_CONFIDENCE_HARD_100.json),
[OCR benchmark report](DECISION_CLOUD_HARD_100_2026-11-07.md) and
[confidence report](DECISION_CONFIDENCE_HARD_100_2026-11-07.md).

The run completed all 100 entries without pipeline failures in 1,144.18 seconds
of per-image time and 1,145.65 seconds of invocation wall time. Its known-usage
API estimate is $0.85635 (about $0.00856 per entry); this is not an invoice or
full operating cost. The runner host was an Apple M2 with 16 GiB memory, but OCR
and decision inference ran at the providers.

| Cloud-default outcome | Selected 100 | TextOCR subset, 81 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tolerant word coverage | 77.19% | 72.74% |
| Exact word coverage | 63.90% | 57.56% |
| Mean image coverage | 68.45% | 63.93% |
| Images at least 80% | 44.00% | 37.04% |
| Images at 100% / full-reference pass | 17.00% | 7.41% |

In the prior full evaluation, the same selected keys scored 75.62% tolerant and
62.63% exact cloud coverage, with 40% at least 80% and 13% full-reference
passes; two entries were unscored due to provider failures. The earlier run used
the prior recovery implementation. This comparison is not controlled and does
not attribute the difference to separate completeness questions, image sizing
or confidence policy.

OpenAI Decisions (`gpt-6-luna`) answered completeness for all 100 cases. On the
79-entry calibration group, choice accuracy against the full-reference pass was
86.1%; four `complete` choices were below 80% tolerant coverage. On the
21-entry held-out group, accuracy was 90.5%, with no `complete` choice below
80%. The reported `confidence` averaged 0.889 and 0.907, respectively; this is
confidence in the selected option, not `P(complete)`. The separate mean native
`P(complete)` was 0.191 and 0.145. Its Brier score against full-reference pass
was 0.098 and 0.061; against at-least-80% coverage it was 0.321 and 0.157.
These small-sample scores are descriptive and do not establish reliability on a
new dataset.

The trial also recorded 160 candidate comparisons, of which 85 had comparable
reference scores. Against tolerant matched-word gain, choice accuracy was
82.5% on calibration and 95.5% on evaluation; the `P(improved)` Brier scores
were 0.040 and 0.048. This comparison ignores geometry and exact transcription,
so it validates only one narrow consequence of the decision. Reviewability,
repair and orientation answers do not have direct objective labels here and
remain descriptive.

No confidence cutoff was fitted or adopted, and production routing is unchanged.
This failure-selected development sample is not an independent benchmark;
TextOCR references come from training annotations. The broader proposed
four-arm question/evidence comparison remains unrun.
